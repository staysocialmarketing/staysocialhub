I have had more conversations about co-buying in the last year than in my entire career before that. The idea makes sense. The execution has details that matter.

Who qualifies together? Most people assume co-buying means a couple. It does not have to be. Two friends, a parent and child, siblings. Lenders look at the combined income, combined credit, and combined debt. The structure works for more people than you would expect.

What happens if one person wants out? This is the question nobody asks until it matters. How does the remaining person qualify on their own? What are the buyout terms? Is there a co-ownership agreement in place? These are conversations I walk every co-buying client through.

Does it actually help with affordability? Combined income means higher qualification. Combined savings means a larger down payment. In the GTA market, that can be the difference between watching from the sidelines and getting into a home.

Co-buying is not a workaround. It is a legitimate strategy that more Canadians are using. It just needs to be done right.
