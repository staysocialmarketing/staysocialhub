What's Shifting

The Bank of Canada held at 2.25% for the sixth consecutive time in July. Bond yields have been stable, and lenders are competing on fixed rates more aggressively than they have in months. If you are shopping, this is a good window.

The next BoC announcement is September 17. Between now and then, the market is watching employment numbers and housing starts for signs of whether the next move is a cut or another hold.

You Asked

"Should I lock in my variable rate or wait?"

It depends on your timeline and your tolerance for movement. If you have two or more years left on your term and you sleep fine with rate changes, staying variable could save you money if the Bank cuts later this year. If certainty matters more than savings, locking in now gives you a known number. There is no universal right answer. There is a right answer for you, and I am happy to run the numbers.
